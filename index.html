Bounce Days: cost and break-even model

Built on Bounce data to 25 Nov 2026 (rider retention, rent, current riders, returns). Costs, benefit prices and usage are owner inputs and assumptions. See Sources.

Additional configs

Journey length variable

Use this to move the starting point away from history. Positive = journeys will be longer anyway (other product work), so the programme gets less credit and costs more, which is the conservative case. Negative = journeys get shorter anyway. Every exit rate changes by the same factor, so the shape of the curve stays as in history.

Returns today

Measured on 6,863 returns (Jun–Sep 2026) against riders who never stopped, at the same Bounce Day. Part of the gap is the kind of rider who leaves and comes back, so the real effect of an offer may be smaller. Untick to treat returners like continuous riders.

Milestones

  1. Rescue benefits: given before renewals. Pay later, Hold EV, EMI week.
  2. Reward benefits: given after renewals. Free day, goodies, gold coins.
  3. One benefit per milestone, no bundles. Less wait time for the next benefit.
  4. Spacing follows the data: weekly to Day 92, while 10–19% of riders leave each week; every two weeks to Day 183; every four weeks after. The Day 7 renewal loses the most riders (12.6%).

Break-even math

Who is counted. Four segments, each rider tagged once by where they were on 7 Dec 2026: new riders (first rental on or after launch, at the weekly rate in Inputs), active riders (renting on 7 Dec, by their Bounce Day), natural returners and extra returners (inactive on 7 Dec, by time away). Every rider follows the same milestones from their current Bounce Day, and the same exit curve (Retention data).
Cost. Welcome benefits (active riders at launch; inactive riders once they come back and pay) plus milestone benefits for everyone. Each benefit costs its cost per use × Used % (Usual benefits).
Margin. Two sources, each rental day counted once. Longer journeys: every exit rate falls by X, so a journey is 1 ÷ (1 − X) times as long. Returns: of riders who end a journey, more come back within 30 days (today 22.2%), resuming at their Bounce Day after about 15 days off; and more riders inactive on 7 Dec come back within 30 days. Extra rental days × margin on an extra day (Inputs).
Break-even. Journey length and return rate are raised by the same %, and the model finds the smallest rise at which the margin from both equals the cost over the output period. "Week by week" repeats this for each period: what it would take to be back at zero by then.
Journey length (projected). 1 ÷ exit rate per rental day, with exit rates taken in Bounce Day bands (1–14, 15–28, 29–91, 92–175, 176+) and combined with fixed weights (active riders at launch), so the mix of new and long-tenure riders doesn't move it. Compared programme vs holdout.
Return rate (30 days). Of riders who stop renting, the share who pay for a plan again within 30 days: riders whose journey ends after launch, and riders inactive on 7 Dec (from launch). Compared programme vs holdout.
Not counted: recovery costs, forfeited deposits and acquisition savings. Returners are assumed to leave at the same rate as continuous riders at the same Bounce Day.

Sources

From Bounce data (business rules applied: excluded cities and bike model, test bikes; checked against the June 2026 figures for submissions, deployments, same-day swaps and renewals):
· Rider retention: share of the 38,269 riders whose first paid rental was on or after 15 Jan 2026 still renting on each Bounce Day, to 24 Nov 2026. Measured to Day 271; after that, 6.8% of remaining riders leave each week (the rate over Days 183–267).
· Rent ₹295.5 per rental day: actual rent for those riders, excluding activation packs, fees and deposits (7-day plans 64% of days at ₹277, 1-day 28% at ₹338).
· Active riders: 10,570 renting on 24 Nov 2026, by lifetime Bounce Days.
· Inactive riders: 42,508 on 25 Nov 2026, by time away, with each group's spread of lifetime Bounce Days.
· Return rates (30 days, no offer): averaged over five reference dates, Jun–Oct 2026: 2–14 days away 17.5%, 15–30 days 8.7%, 31–60 days 4.8%, 61–180 days 2.6%, 181+ days 0.7%. 22.2% for riders 2–7 days after a journey ends.
· Returners leave faster: exit rate in the first 8 weeks back, 6,863 returns Jun–Sep 2026, ×1.7 to ×3.9 against continuous riders at the same Bounce Day.
· New riders: 923 a week, the average of the last 12 complete weeks to 22 Nov 2026, leaving out the 4 festive weeks (19 Oct – 15 Nov, about 1,270–1,440 a week).
Owner inputs: costs per rental day (energy ₹80, maintenance ₹20, bike ₹100); launch date 7 Dec 2026; welcome benefits; milestone schedule; goodie prices (holder ₹150, raincoat ₹300, helmet ₹400, gold ₹200).
Assumptions (the benefits don't exist yet, so there's no data): Used % for each benefit (hold 50%, pay later 30%, free day 90%, EMI 17.5%, goodies 100%); 10% of pay-later days never paid; EMI cost only when a daily rider switches to a 7-day plan; returners spend about 15 days off before coming back. Benefit costs use actual prices: free day ₹295.5, pay later at the 1-day price ₹338, EMI week 7 × ₹338 − ₹1,938 = ₹430, hold ₹195.5 − ₹150 fee = ₹46.
